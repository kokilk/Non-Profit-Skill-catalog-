# The minutes form

Read this at steps 1–6, before structuring notes into minutes and whenever a motion, conflict, executive session or sensitive detail comes up.

## 1. The form

Use `assets/minutes-template.md`: a header (organization, body, date, time, place), attendance and quorum, call to order, approval of prior minutes, reports, old and new business, conflicts of interest, executive session, action items, next meeting, adjournment, an approval line and a secretary signature line. Keep the **DRAFT: not approved** banner at the top until the secretary records an approval date.

**Principle:** minutes record what was **done**. Reports get a one-line summary of what was presented. Discussion isn't recorded, and opinions aren't attributed, unless the board asks for it.

## 2. Motions

Each motion gets its own `### Motion M-n:` block with four lines: **Moved by**, **Seconded by**, **Vote** (tally, or "voice vote" when no count was taken), **Outcome** (carried, failed, tabled, withdrawn). If any of them isn't in the notes, write `[CONFIRM: …]`. "All in favor" with 8 present can be written "8 yes, 0 no" only if the notes say everyone present voted yes.

Tallies must add up to the members present, counting abstentions and recusals. If someone left the room, record that.

## 3. Conflicts, executive session and Red content

- **Conflicts of interest:** record who declared a conflict, on which item, and that they recused. Every recusal in a vote needs a declaration here.
- **Executive session:** record only the times (entered, left) and the topic **category** (personnel, legal, real estate). Nothing about what was discussed.
- **Red content** (an employee's health, a client's situation, a donor's personal circumstances): leave it out of the minutes. Say so to the user and suggest the item belongs in executive session with a general description.

## 4. Recordings and transcripts

If the notes came from a recording or an auto-transcript, remind the user to confirm that everyone present consented to being recorded. **Rules differ by place: tell the user to check their own rules or ask counsel; don't state what the law requires.** Transcripts also need extra care: drop side conversations and personal remarks, and keep only actions and decisions.
