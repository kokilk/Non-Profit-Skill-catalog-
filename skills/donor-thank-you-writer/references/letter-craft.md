# Letter craft

Read this at steps 1–2 (voice and impact facts) and step 6 (drafting), and for anonymous, in-honor or memorial gifts.

## 1. Voice notes

From 2–3 sample letters, write 5–8 short notes the user approves: greeting and sign-off style, sentence length, formality, words they use ("neighbors", "families"), words they avoid, how they describe their work, who signs. If they have no samples, ask three questions: how formal, who signs, one phrase that sounds like them.

## 2. Impact facts

Ask for 3–6 statements that are **true and current**, each with a source: "This year our tutors met with 312 students." Store them in `impact-facts.json` (`assets/impact-facts-template.json`) with IDs (I-01 …). These are the only impact claims a letter may make. Their numbers are the only numbers `lint` accepts besides the gift's own amount and date. Don't round, combine or upgrade them ("over 300" is fine only if the fact says so).

## 3. Letter rules

- **Open with the person and the gift**, not the organization.
- **Be specific about what gifts like theirs do**, using a supplied fact. Prefer "gifts like yours help…" to claiming one gift caused an outcome.
- **One next touch:** an invitation, an update to come, a thank-you call. No new ask unless the org's practice includes one.
- **Short, warm, human:** about 120–250 words, reading level around grade 8.
- **Match the voice notes.**
- **Segments:** first gift (welcome them), repeat (acknowledge their continued support without inventing totals), lapsed and returning (welcome back, no guilt), major (more personal; a call or visit offered by a named person), recurring (thank them for ongoing support; don't state a cumulative total unless the record has it).

## 4. Anonymous, honor and memorial gifts

- **Anonymous** usually means "don't name me publicly". Still thank the donor personally, by name if the record has one, and never list them in public recognition. If the record has no name at all, don't invent one: ask how the org addresses such donors.
- **In honor of:** thank the donor and name the honoree exactly as in the record. Don't assume the relationship ("your mother") unless the record says it. A notice to the honoree is separate and needs its own address.
- **In memory of:** gentle and brief. Name the person as recorded. Don't describe the death, the illness or the family's circumstances. The notice to the family is a separate letter, and only if the org has the family's address and practice.
